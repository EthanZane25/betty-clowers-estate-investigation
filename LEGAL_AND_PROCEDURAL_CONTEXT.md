# Georgia Legal and Procedural Context

**Research aid only; not legal advice. Current statutes and procedural deadlines should be verified by Georgia probate counsel.**

## 1. Will Execution — O.C.G.A. § 53-4-20

Georgia law provides that a will must be in writing, signed by the testator (or another person in the testator's presence and at express direction), and attested/subscribed in the testator's presence by two or more competent witnesses.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-4/article-3/section-53-4-20/

Investigation relevance:
- identify both witnesses;
- establish location and signing sequence;
- determine whether statutory execution requirements were satisfied;
- verify the self-proving/notarial block separately.

## 2. Testamentary Capacity — O.C.G.A. § 53-4-11

Georgia states that testamentary capacity exists when the testator has a decided and rational desire as to disposition of property. Advanced age or weakness of intellect alone does not necessarily eliminate capacity.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-4/article-2/section-53-4-11/

Investigation relevance:
- capacity is especially important at the time of execution;
- focus medical and witness evidence on August 4, 2025.

## 3. Freedom of Volition / Undue Influence — O.C.G.A. § 53-4-12

A will must be freely and voluntarily executed. Georgia states that a will is invalid if another person's will is substituted for the testator's wishes through fraud, misrepresentation, duress, or undue influence.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-4/article-2/section-53-4-12/

## 4. Caregiver / Beneficiary Participation — Creamer v. Manley

In *Creamer v. Manley*, A24A0111 (Ga. Ct. App. Mar. 14, 2024), the beneficiary was a friend/caregiver, executrix, and majority beneficiary.

The court explained that a rebuttable presumption of undue influence can arise when a beneficiary:
- occupies a confidential relationship;
- is not the natural object of the testator's bounty; and
- actively participates in planning, preparing, or executing the will.

The caregiver prevailed because the record did not show her active participation in the will process.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/court-of-appeals/2024/a24a0111.html

Investigation relevance:
- determine whether Frances actively participated in obtaining the 2025 will;
- caregiver status plus benefit is important context but is not, standing alone, proof of undue influence.

## 5. Prior Wills as Evidence — In re Estate of Flournoy

Georgia appellate authority recognizes that prior wills may be relevant in an undue-influence case because they can shed light on testamentary capacity or undue influence in procuring a later will.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/court-of-appeals/2024/a24a0702.html

- the preserved April 1, 2008 source photographs are high-value comparative evidence; obtaining an original/certified/authenticated copy remains a provenance priority.

## 6. Solemn-Form Service — O.C.G.A. § 53-5-22

Georgia's solemn-form statute requires notice to the testator's heirs. A known heir residing in Georgia is generally to be personally served at least 30 days before probate unless service is waived.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-5/article-3/section-53-5-22/

## 7. Probate Jurisdiction and Domicile — O.C.G.A. § 53-5-1

Georgia probate courts have exclusive jurisdiction over probate of wills, and the county of the testator's domicile at death generally determines the Georgia probate venue. The statute also contains a rebuttable rule concerning domicile for a person in a nursing home or similar facility.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-5/article-1/section-53-5-1/

## 8. Inventory — O.C.G.A. § 53-7-30

Unless otherwise provided by the will or the personal representative is relieved under applicable law, Georgia generally requires the personal representative to prepare and file an inventory and provide it to beneficiaries/heirs within six months after qualification.

Source:
https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-7/article-3/section-53-7-30/

## 10. Public Records Are Not Certified Copies

The Georgia Probate Records estate page warns that online documents are not certified. Certified copies must be obtained from Bibb County Probate Court.

Maintain a separate distinction among:
- official web copy;
- certified court copy;
- family copy;
- reconstructed summary.
