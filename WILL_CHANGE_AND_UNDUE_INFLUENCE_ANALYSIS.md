# Will Change and Undue-Influence Evidence Framework

This file identifies what is documented, what is not established, and which evidence matters most under Georgia will-contest law. It is an investigation aid, not legal advice.

## Documented factual sequence

1. Preserved source photographs show a materially different April 1, 2008 will in which Frances Brooks Thornton is not named.
2. The August 4, 2025 will describes Frances as Betty's "daughter and caretaker."
3. The 2025 will gives Frances Betty's interest in 1661 Lawton Avenue and directs Frances to sell the property.

## Correct characterization

The evidence supports saying:

> Betty's estate plan materially changed in Frances's favor during a period in which the later will describes Frances as Betty's caretaker, and Frances later became executor after the first nominee declined.

The evidence does **not yet establish**:

> Frances personally changed, procured, directed, drafted, coerced, or unduly influenced Betty's will.

The second proposition requires evidence.

## Georgia legal framework

### Testamentary capacity — O.C.G.A. § 53-4-11
Georgia states that testamentary capacity exists when the testator has a "decided and rational desire" concerning disposition of property. The critical inquiry is capacity at the time the will was executed.

Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-4/article-2/section-53-4-11/

### Freedom of volition / undue influence — O.C.G.A. § 53-4-12
A will must be freely and voluntarily executed. Georgia provides that a will is invalid when another person's will is substituted for the testator's wishes through fraud, misrepresentation, duress, or undue influence.

Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-4/article-2/section-53-4-12/

### Execution — O.C.G.A. § 53-4-20
A Georgia will generally must be in writing, signed by or at the direction of the testator, and attested/subscribed in the testator's presence by two or more competent witnesses.

Source: https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-53/chapter-4/article-3/section-53-4-20/

## Especially relevant Georgia appellate authority

### Creamer v. Manley, A24A0111 (Ga. Ct. App. March 14, 2024)

The case involved a friend/caregiver who was named executrix and received the majority of the testator's estate.

The Court of Appeals explained that a rebuttable presumption of undue influence may arise when a beneficiary:
1. occupies a confidential relationship with the testator;
2. is not the natural object of the testator's bounty; and
3. takes an active part in planning, preparing, or executing the will.

The caregiver prevailed there because the record lacked evidence that she participated in the will's planning, preparation, or execution.

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/court-of-appeals/2024/a24a0111.html

**Investigation implication:** evidence of Frances's actual participation in obtaining the 2025 will is much more probative than caregiver status alone.

### In re Estate of Flournoy, A24A0702 (Ga. Ct. App. 2024)

The Court of Appeals recognized that prior wills can be relevant in an undue-influence case because they may shed light on mental capacity or on whether a subsequent will was procured through undue influence.

Source: https://law.justia.com/cases/georgia/court-of-appeals/2024/a24a0702.html

**Investigation implication:** the preserved 2008 source photographs now provide direct comparative evidence of the earlier estate plan. Obtaining an original, attorney-retained, certified, or otherwise authenticated copy would strengthen provenance.

## Evidence matrix

### A. Confidential/caregiver relationship
Determine:
- whether Frances was paid;
- employer/agency or direct-pay arrangement;
- schedule and duration;
- services performed;
- whether Betty depended on Frances for transportation, medication, finances, food, communication, housing, or appointments;
- whether Frances held POA or account authority.

### B. Active participation in the will
Determine:
- who first contacted the lawyer;
- who selected the lawyer;
- who scheduled each appointment;
- who drove or accompanied Betty;
- who provided names, addresses, property information, and beneficiary information;
- who paid;
- who communicated with counsel before/after execution;
- who received drafts;
- whether Frances was present during legal advice or execution;
- who selected witnesses and notary;
- who retained the original.

### C. Capacity at execution
Target the period around August 4, 2025:
- physician and nursing notes;
- medication administration records;
- cognitive/mental-status findings;
- delirium, infection, dehydration, pain, oxygen issues;
- sedating medication;
- hospital/facility transfers;
- social-work/case-management notes;
- attorney observations;
- witness observations.

### D. Natural objects of bounty and family knowledge
Determine what the drafting lawyer was told about:
- Kayveon/Keyvean;
- Tiffany;
- other people in the 2008 plan;
- Betty's prior will;
- relationship between Betty and Frances.

### E. Parallel financial changes
Look for changes near the will date:
- joint accounts;
- POD/TOD;
- life insurance;
- retirement/annuity beneficiaries;
- large withdrawals;
- new loans;
- deeds;
- POA;
- account signers.

### F. Control after death
Track:
- possession of the original will;
- funeral/cremation arrangements;
- property access;
- estate funds;
- property listing/sale efforts;
- asset collection;
- creditor payments;
- distributions.

## Do not overstate

A suspicious sequence, caregiver relationship, or beneficial change is a reason to investigate. It is not itself proof of fraud, exploitation, or undue influence.
