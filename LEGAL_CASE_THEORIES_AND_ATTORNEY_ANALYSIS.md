# Legal Case Theories and Attorney-Style Analysis

> Working legal-analysis notes. This is not a source document and does not establish disputed facts. Evidence files control.

## 1. Undue influence (main contest theory)

Georgia requires a will to be freely and voluntarily executed. It is invalid if another person’s will is substituted for the testator’s. OCGA § 53-4-12. The presumption lawyers chase is from Bailey v. Edmundson and Bean v. Wilson\:A rebuttable presumption of undue influence arises when the beneficiary:

1. occupies a confidential relationship with the testator, 
2. is not the natural object of the bounty, and 
3. takes an active part in planning, preparing, or executing the will. 

Georgia caregiver cases look a lot like this fact pattern: live-in or paid caregiver, house left to the caregiver, prior family will displaced, questions about who called the lawyer and who sat in the room. Bean v. Wilson is the closest published analog. Facts in the file that a contestant’s lawyer would put in the first paragraph:

- 2008 will source photographs: multiple family remaindermen, different executors, burial instructions, and Frances is not named in the four-page copy. 
- August 4, 2025 will: Frances called “daughter and caretaker,” receives Betty’s interest in Lawton Avenue, is told to sell it, and is successor personal representative. 
- Execution about 81 days before death. 
- Family reports Frances was a paid caregiver, not a legal daughter. 
- Petition later says she is not biological or adopted and is a goddaughter. 
- After Dave Dennis III declined, Frances both takes the asset and administers the estate.

Facts a defense lawyer would put in the first paragraph:

- Confidential relationship plus opportunity is not enough. 
- The presumption does not arise unless Frances actively participated in procuring the instrument. Georgia courts have granted judgment for the beneficiary where the caregiver did not arrange the lawyer, was not in the conference, and was not present at signing. 
- Current records do not prove Frances arranged the appointment, transported Betty, paid the lawyer, reviewed drafts, picked witnesses, or retained the original. 
- Naming Dave Dennis III as first personal representative is evidence the plan was not “Frances takes control of everything.” 
- “Daughter” can be honorific. Goddaughter plus long care is a recognized natural-object argument, even if weaker than a biological child. 
- Georgia allows an adult child to be disinherited. There is no forced share for Kayveon if the will is valid.

The fight will be discovery into the drafting file: intake sheet, who called, who paid, who was in the room, capacity notes, drafts, emails with Frances, and witness/notary ties to Frances.

## 2. Testamentary capacity

Capacity in Georgia is modest: a “decided and rational desire” as to disposition of property. Age, illness, and eccentricity do not defeat a will by themselves. OCGA § 53-4-11. Capacity is measured at the moment of signing. Counsel would still collect:

- Piedmont Macon records around August 4, 2025 
- PruittHealth–Augusta Hills admission, cognitive screens, responsible-party forms 
- medications, delirium, dementia workups, social-work notes naming the caregiver 
- who brought Betty to the lawyer and whether she spoke independently

The PruittHealth creditor claim for last illness is not proof of incapacity. It is proof she was in institutional care near the end, which supports both a capacity investigation and the domicile/nursing-home analysis below.

## 3. The “daughter” vs. “goddaughter” contradiction

This is not a technicality. Lawyers would use it three ways:

1. Fraud / misrepresentation. If someone caused the will to recast a paid caregiver as a daughter, that can attack free volition under § 53-4-12. 
2. Credibility. The petition walks the relationship back. That is impeachment of the propounder’s narrative. 
3. Drafting source. If the lawyer’s notes say “client said this is my daughter,” vs. “caregiver supplied the family tree,” that goes to active procurement.

It is also a defense point: testators often use family language for people who raised or cared for them. The petition’s correction can be spun as candor, not concealment.

## 4. Domicile and whether Bibb County had jurisdiction

This is one of the sharpest issues in the file, and it is easy to miss.

- 2008 will: Betty is a Bibb County, Georgia resident. 
- 2025 will: she is domiciled in Aiken County, South Carolina. 
- Probate was opened in Bibb County. 
- Cremation reportedly occurred in South Carolina. 
- Last-illness claim is from a facility in the Augusta area.

Georgia probate jurisdiction follows domicile at death, not where the house sits. OCGA § 53-5-1. If she was in a nursing home, domicile is presumed to be the county she lived in before entering the facility, unless rebutted. If she died a Georgia domiciliary, Bibb may be correct. If she died an SC domiciliary, original GA probate of an out-of-state will is a different statute, and SC may be the domiciliary forum. Counsel would pull voter rolls, driver’s license, tax returns, Medicare/Medicaid address, facility face sheets, and the death certificate.A venue/jurisdiction defect can be used to attack the entire Bibb proceeding, independent of undue-influence proof.

## 5. Service of process and the April 22 order

Heirs of a Georgia decedent generally must be personally served for solemn form. Leaving papers with a third party is a classic defective-service fact pattern. If Kayveon was not lawfully served:

- the finding that “heirs were served” is vulnerable 
- the admission order may be void or voidable as to him 
- a caveat may still be timely

If he was served and sat out the 30 days, the will-contest ship may have sailed, and counsel would shift to PR removal, accounting, and inter vivos claims.

## 6. Title to 1661 Lawton Avenue

The 2025 will only gives Frances “Betty’s interest.” That is not the same as “Frances owns the house.”Issues a real-estate / probate lawyer would raise:

- 2008 plan: life estate to Donald Wright, remainders to Keyvean, Tiffany Clowers Hall, Andre Washington, Dave Dennis III, Starla Wright, Bertha Anderson. 
- 2009 reported $0 quitclaim — possible title cleanup after Willie Lee Clowers’s 2004 death, or a real transfer. The deed is not in hand. 
- Whether Betty still owned fee simple, a remainder, or only a fractional interest in 2025. 
- Whether Frances already appears on any deed. 
- Liens, tax deeds, and whether a will direction to “sell” is valid if others hold recorded interests.

A will cannot convey what the decedent did not own. Title work can shrink or destroy the gift even if the will stands.

## 7. Conflicted personal representative

Frances is:

- the principal devisee of the house interest 
- directed to sell it 
- now the person with letters testamentary

Even if the will is valid, Georgia law still requires a PR to inventory, account, give notice to creditors, and avoid self-dealing. Counsel for an heir can petition for:

- inventory and formal accounting 
- bond 
- removal for conflict, mismanagement, or hostility 
- a limitation on her selling to herself or a related buyer 
- surcharge if assets disappear

Dave Dennis III’s February 6, 2026 declination is its own deposition topic: who asked him to step aside, what he was told, and whether he knew about the 2008 plan or Betty’s condition.

---

## 8. Life insurance and other non-probate assets

Family reports of cash-outs are only reported. They still matter because beneficiary changes and surrenders can be attacked even if the will is admitted. Georgia has applied will-contest undue-influence analysis to beneficiary designations. Counsel would subpoena every carrier for ownership history, beneficiary changes, POA documents, and where the check went.

---

## 9. Cremation versus the 2008 burial instructions

The 2008 will contained burial language. The later plan and a South Carolina cremation cut the other way. That usually does not void a will by itself. It is evidence of:

- who had control at death 
- who claimed authority to sign a cremation authorization 
- whether family was excluded

Useful for the isolation / control narrative, and separately for any funeral-contract or remains dispute.

---

## 10. Possible POA / financial-control claims

The repo correctly lists as open questions whether Frances had a power of attorney, controlled accounts, or was the “responsible party” at the facility. Those facts support:

- confidential-relationship proof 
- possible inter vivos conversion or constructive-trust claims 
- a request for bank and facility records

They do not, on this file, establish a crime. A careful lawyer would not plead criminal elder abuse as if it were proven.

2. Full deed chain for 1661 Lawton Avenue, especially the 2009 instrument

4. Medical records around August 4, 2025 
5. Insurance company files 
6. Cremation authorization and death certificate

Until those exist, the legally honest position is the one the repo already takes: there is a material change in the estate plan during a caregiver relationship, plus documentary contradictions and service/domicile problems. That is enough for a lawyer to investigate and, if service is still open, to file a caveat. It is not, by itself, proof that Frances procured the 2025 will.

## How the two sides would actually frame the case

**Contestant frame:** paid caregiver, confidential relationship, unnatural disposition, radical departure from the 2008 family will, false or inflated “daughter” label later walked back, execution close to death while in declining health, then the caregiver becomes both devisee and executor after the named PR steps aside. Compare Bean and Bailey.

**Propounder frame:** goddaughter/caretaker whom Betty chose to reward; “daughter” is language of affection; first executor was a third party; no proof Frances drafted or steered the lawyer; Georgia capacity is low; adult son can be left out; solemn form already granted.

The first question any competent lawyer would answer, before writing a long undue-influence brief, is narrower: was Kayveon validly served before April 22, 2026, and is Bibb County the right court? Those two points decide whether this is still a will contest or only a fight about administration and title.
